# Princeton, Massachusetts — Hotels & Boarding Houses of the Resort Era, 1887–1890

An interactive historical map and guide to the lodging places and civic landmarks of Princeton, Massachusetts during its heyday as a summer resort on the slopes of Mount Wachusett.

**▶ Live map: https://notesfromtheabstract.github.io/princeton-1887-1890-map/**

After the Civil War, the quiet farming town of Princeton became one of central Massachusetts' most fashionable summer resorts, drawing city dwellers to the cool, clear air of Mount Wachusett. At its height in the late 1880s the town's population swelled by roughly 2,500 tourists every summer, most staying for weeks or the whole season. This project maps the lodging places standing in 1887–1890, set against a period reconstruction of the town.

## What's on the map

- **Hotels** (red) — the town-centre houses (Wachusett House, its Tavern & Annex, and the Prospect House / Princeton Inn), the mountain-slope hotels (Mountain House, Grand View, Mount Pleasant House), and the Summit House atop Wachusett.
- **Boarding houses & guesthouses** (green) — Harrington Farm, Pratt's Cottage, the Rufus Davis boarding house, and Fernside.
- **Civic & industrial landmarks** (gold) — the First Congregational Church (moved east of the Common in 1887), Bagg Hall / Town Hall (1884), the Goodnow Memorial Library (1883), the cemeteries, and East Princeton's mill village: the Temple & Stuart Chair Factory, Mechanics Hall, and Whitcomb's store & post office.
- **The three villages** — Princeton Centre, West Village (Pratt's Corner), and the East Princeton chair-mill village.
- **Period landscape** — the through-roads, the Boston, Barre & Gardner Railroad (1871) and its depot, the town boundary, the ponds, real elevation contours of Mount Wachusett, and the village buildings.

## How to use it

- The map opens on the **1887–1890 reconstruction**. Use the **layers control** (top-right) to switch to the modern map, or the **opacity slider** to fade the historic layer over the modern one for comparison.
- **Click** any pin, building, or contour for details.
- The left panel is a scrollable directory of every lodging place; click an entry to fly to it.

## Methodology & accuracy

This is a **reconstruction, not a scanned original map.** Road, rail, water, and building geometry come from present-day OpenStreetMap data, filtered to the features documented before 1890 and styled in period fashion; elevation contours are computed from real terrain data. Dated landmarks (church, town hall, library, chair factory, etc.) and the lodging houses are individually verified against town histories and placed from their historic addresses.

Because no dataset records which individual houses stood in 1887, the **building footprints are present-day outlines**, shaded to distinguish those inside the documented historic-district cores (largely pre-1900) from outlying buildings whose individual dates are unverified. For per-house, owner-named accuracy, a georeferenced period atlas plate (the 1870 Beers or 1898 Richards *Atlas of Worcester County*) would be the next step.
